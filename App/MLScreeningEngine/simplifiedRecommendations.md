### **Anxiety**
*   **Diaphragmatic (Belly) Breathing:** Inhale through the nose to expand the abdomen, followed by a slow exhale to calm the nervous system.[1, 2]
*   **Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR):** Systematically tense and then release muscle groups from your toes to your face to recognize and lower physical tension.[2, 3]
*   **Catch it, Check it, Change it:** Identify an anxious thought, check the evidence for its accuracy, and change it to a more balanced perspective.[4]
*   **Worry Scheduling:** Set a dedicated 15-minute "worry time" during the day to process concerns, allowing you to postpone anxiety at other times.[5, 6]
*   **5-4-3-2-1 Grounding:** Name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, and 1 you taste to interrupt spiraling thoughts.[7, 8]
*   **Mindfulness "Just Noticing":** Observe your thoughts like clouds passing in the sky without judging or trying to change them.[9, 10]

### **Depression**
*   **Activity Monitoring:** Track your hourly activities and rate your mood ($0-10$) to identify which behaviors help or hurt your emotional state.[11]
*   **Pleasure and Mastery Scheduling:** Purposefully schedule at least one activity daily that you enjoy (pleasure) and one that makes you feel capable (mastery).[11, 12]
*   **Breaking Goals into Micro-steps:** Divide overwhelming tasks, like "cleaning the house," into tiny, manageable steps like "washing 5 plates".[13, 12]
*   **Opposite Action:** When you feel the urge to isolate, deliberately reach out to one person or engage in a small social interaction.[14, 15]
*   **Structured Movement:** Engage in brief, daily physical activity (like a 10-minute walk) to naturally boost brain chemicals that lift mood.[16, 17]
*   **Assertive Communication:** Practice "DEAR MAN" skills (Describe, Express, Assert, Reinforce) to communicate needs clearly and reduce relationship-based distress.[18]

### **ADHD**
*   **The "Launch Pad":** Designate one specific spot for essential items like keys, wallet, and glasses to reduce morning stress and memory load.[19]
*   **1-3-5 Prioritization:** Each day, select 1 big task, 3 medium tasks, and 5 small tasks to prevent feeling overwhelmed by a master list.[19]
*   **Visual Timers:** Use external clocks or timers to stay aware of passing time and manage "time blindness" during tasks.[20]
*   **Stop. Think. Act.:** Practice pausing and taking a deep breath before making a decision to improve impulse control.[21]
*   **Reward Systems:** Set up immediate, small rewards for completing difficult or boring tasks to maintain motivation.[21, 22]
*   **Environmental Cues:** Use sticky notes or digital reminders in places you cannot miss them to support your working memory.[19, 21]

### **Sleep / Insomnia**
*   **The 15-20 Minute Rule:** If you are not asleep within 20 minutes, get out of bed and do a quiet activity in another room until you feel sleepy again.[23, 24]
*   **Consistent Wake-up Times:** Wake up at the same time every day, including weekends, to regulate your internal circadian clock.[23, 25]
*   **Stimulus Control:** Use your bed only for sleep and intimacy; avoid working, eating, or using screens while in bed.[23, 24]
*   **Sleep Efficiency Calculation:** Use a sleep diary to ensure your time in bed matches your actual sleep ability: $SE = \frac{Total\ Sleep\ Time}{Total\ Time\ in\ Bed} \times 100$.[24]
*   **Paradoxical Intention:** Instead of "trying" to fall asleep, lie down and calmly try to stay awake to reduce performance anxiety about sleep.[6]
*   **Daily "Worry Time":** Process stressful thoughts during the afternoon so they don't resurface when you hit the pillow at night.[6]

### **Stress**
*   **Aerobic De-escalation:** Engage in high-intensity movement (like jogging or jumping jacks) for 10 minutes to burn off excess stress hormones like cortisol.[26, 27]
*   **Gratitude Listing:** Write down three specific things you are grateful for each day to shift your brain's focus from threats to resources.[7]
*   **Boundary Setting:** Practice saying "No" to extra commitments to prevent burnout and protect your mental energy.[1]
*   **4-7-8 Breathing:** Inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 7, and exhale for 8 to physically force your body into a state of relaxation.[28, 29]
*   **Mindful Walking:** Walk without distractions, focusing entirely on the sensation of your feet hitting the ground and the colors in your environment.[30, 1]
*   **Savoring Senses:** Take a few moments to fully experience a pleasant scent, flavor, or texture to anchor yourself in the present.[7, 30]

### **Social Anxiety**
*   **Fear Ladder Construction:** Rank social situations by difficulty (0-100 SUDS) and start with the easiest ones first.[31, 32]
*   **Dropping Safety Behaviors:** Identify subtle habits (like checking your phone or avoiding eye contact) and purposefully stop using them during interactions.[15, 33]
*   **Small-Step Exposures:** Practice low-stakes interactions, such as asking a store clerk a question or offering a compliment to a stranger.[34]
*   **Social Skills Role-play:** Rehearse specific social scenarios (e.g., introducing yourself or making small talk) with a trusted person.[5, 18]
*   **Spotlight Reframing:** Remind yourself that others are generally more focused on their own performance than on judging yours.[9, 33]

### **Trauma**
*   **Sensory Grounding:** Use the 5-4-3-2-1 technique immediately if you feel a flashback or dissociation beginning.[7, 30]
*   **Safe Place Imagery:** Visualize a peaceful location in detail, focusing on the calming sounds, smells, and sensations associated with it.[35, 36]
*   **The "Container" Exercise:** Mentally place distressing images or memories into a secure, locked container to be addressed only during therapy.[37, 38]
*   **Butterfly Hug:** Cross your arms and alternately tap your shoulders to provide soothing bilateral stimulation during moments of distress.[1, 29]
*   **Temperature Modulation:** Splash cold water on your face or hold an ice cube to rapidly bring your attention back to the present moment.[39, 26]
*   **Anchoring Statement:** Recite your name, the current date, and your physical location out loud to reinforce current safety.[7, 30]

### **General Distress**
*   **STOP Skill:** **S**top, **T**ake a step back, **O**bserve, and **P**roceed mindfully to avoid impulsive reactions.[40, 41]
*   **TIPP Protocol:** Use **T**emperature (cold water), **I**ntense exercise, **P**aced breathing, and **P**aired muscle relaxation for rapid biological reset.[26, 40]
*   **Radical Acceptance:** Acknowledge a painful reality as it is, without fighting it, to prevent pain from turning into long-term suffering.[40, 42]
*   **ACCEPTS Distraction:** Use **A**ctivities, **C**ontributing to others, or **P**ushing away distressing thoughts to survive a crisis.[42, 43]
*   **Leaves on a Stream:** Visualize your distressing thoughts as leaves floating down a stream, watching them drift past without getting "hooked".[27, 44]
