# MSPSS — Social Support Screening Guide

## Overview
The Multidimensional Scale of Perceived Social Support (MSPSS) is a brief screening tool used to measure an individual's perception of their social support network. It is frequently employed in clinical and research settings to evaluate how supported a person feels across three primary interpersonal domains over recent general timeframes. Strong social support is often tied to better mental health outcomes.

## What It Measures
The scale breaks social support down into three distinct sub-categories:
- **Family (Fam)**: Support derived from immediate or extended family members.
- **Friends (Fri)**: Support derived from peers, social groups, and friends.
- **Significant Other (SO)**: Support derived from a romantic partner or another single deeply trusted individual.

## Score Interpretation
The questionnaire uses a 7-point Likert scale (1 = Very Strongly Disagree to 7 = Very Strongly Agree) across 12 items. 
Scores can be calculated as the sum of all items (ranging from 12 to 84) or as an average score per item (ranging from 1 to 7).

*(Based on Mean Scores 1 to 7):*
- **1.0 – 2.9**: Low perceived social support
- **3.0 – 5.0**: Moderate perceived social support  
- **5.1 – 7.0**: High perceived social support  

## What These Scores Mean
Unlike traditional symptom scales (like the GAD-7 or PHQ-9) where high scores are concerning, **higher scores on the MSPSS indicate stronger perceived social support**, which serves as a protective factor for emotional well-being. Lower scores indicate feelings of isolation, loneliness, or an unsupportive environment.

## Safety & Support Note
A significantly low score on the MSPSS often acts as a risk multiplier for other mental health challenges, such as depression or anxiety. If an individual feels chronically unsupported:
- Engaging with community groups, support groups, or group therapy may be highly beneficial.
- It highlights the critical importance of building a strong therapeutic alliance with the clinician.

## Important Notes
- Results reflect *perceived* support, which may differ from the actual number of relationships an individual has.
- Low support in one domain (e.g., Family) can often be successfully buffered by high support in another domain (e.g., Friends).
- Results are informational and highlight areas for potential psychosocial intervention.
