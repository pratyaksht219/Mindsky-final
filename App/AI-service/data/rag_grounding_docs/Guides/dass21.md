# DASS-21 — Depression, Anxiety, and Stress Guide

## Overview
The DASS-21 is a self-report instrument that measures three related emotional states: depression, anxiety, and stress. It is commonly used to assess overall emotional distress and to differentiate between symptom patterns. The scale reflects experiences over the past week and is widely used in both clinical and research contexts.

## What It Measures
- **Depression**: Low mood, hopelessness, lack of interest
- **Anxiety**: Fear, panic symptoms, tension
- **Stress**: Irritability, pressure, difficulty relaxing

## Score Interpretation
Each domain is classified as:
- Normal
- Mild
- Moderate
- Severe
- Extremely Severe

## What These Scores Mean
Elevated scores suggest higher emotional strain in one or more areas.

## Safety & Support Note
If emotional distress feels intense, persistent, or overwhelming:
- Reach out to supportive people
- Consider professional mental health care
- Seek urgent help if safety concerns arise

## Important Notes
- Emotional states can overlap and fluctuate.
- Scores reflect current experiences, not diagnoses.
